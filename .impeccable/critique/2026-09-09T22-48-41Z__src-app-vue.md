## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment**: This reads as authored for Taiwanese canyoning trip-planning, not a skinned template. The strongest evidence is the water-station popup leading with a verdict sentence built from real WRA alert thresholds ("距離二級警戒還有5.56m") rather than a bare reading, plus grade tooltips written in canyoning register ("垂降 V3｜落差大或地形複雜") instead of translated hiking terms. Where it slips toward generic is the visual language itself — indigo-on-navy pill-and-card chrome is a very common "modern dashboard" look; the domain specificity currently lives in content and interaction, not yet in a distinctive visual identity.

**Deterministic scan**: `detect.mjs` ran in **degraded mode** (the skill's own `node_modules` is missing `htmlparser2`/`css-select`/`css-tree`/`domutils`, so it fell back to regex-only matching with contrast/selector evaluation off) and returned 0 findings from the CLI pass — an undercount by the tool's own admission, not a clean bill. The browser-injected `detect.js` overlay (which does compute real contrast/font metrics against the rendered DOM) told a different story: **87–98 anti-patterns** across the three inspected views, concentrated in three rules —
- `undersized-ui-text` ×70 (labels like "瀑高" and the bottom-bar "搜尋"/"設定" rendering at **10.4px**)
- `low-contrast` ×13 (`h2.title` at 2.5:1, `.route-cluster` badges at 2.8:1, both under WCAG AA's 4.5:1)
- `text-occlusion` ×~20 (numbered map markers 42–92% covered once any panel is open)

plus one-off `clipped-overflow-container`, `cramped-padding`, and `gpt-thin-border-wide-shadow` flags that Assessment B itself identified as **likely false positives**: two are Leaflet's own library-managed full-bleed container behaving as designed, one is a `position:fixed` element that a `overflow:hidden` ancestor cannot actually clip (no containing-block property set), and the "0.67px border" is a devicePixelRatio/zoom measurement artifact of a CSS `1px solid` border, not a real hairline.

**Visual overlays**: Browser mutation and injection succeeded; the overlay was rendered live and inspected in that session's tab, then the tab and the temporary live-server were closed before this report was written — no overlay is currently left open in your browser. The text-occlusion and undersized-text findings above are the console summary of what that overlay painted.

## Overall Impression

The product-specific thinking is real and good — the water-station verdict card is the actual answer to this app's reason for existing, and the grading vocabulary is authentic. What's holding the score back isn't taste, it's execution details that directly undercut the app's own stated goal (a 30-second go/no-go read): text small enough to need a second look, contrast that fails on the very brand color, and two independent floating-panel systems that overlap instead of taking turns. The biggest opportunity is surfacing the water-station verdict *earlier* in the flow (see Questions below) rather than leaving the app's best feature one navigation step away.

## What's Working

1. **`WaterStationDetail.vue`'s status card** — a tone-coded verdict ("不建議進入溪谷" / distance-to-next-alert in meters) before any raw chart data. This is literally what "30-second decision" means in this domain.
2. **`DifficultyGuide.vue`** — real content built around this community's V/A/T/star system with drill/undeveloped tags, not a generic help modal.
3. **Grade tooltips in `RouteDetail.vue`** (`ROPE_TIPS`/`WATER_TIPS`/`TIME_TIPS`) — explain what a V3 or A2 means right where the decision gets made.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] UI text renders too small to read at a glance.** Detector-confirmed: 70 elements at **10.4px**, including the "瀑高" (drop height) data label and the bottom-bar "搜尋"/"設定" labels — the exact numbers and nav actions a user needs in the 30-second pre-trip check. Why it matters: this directly undercuts Product Principle #2 ("30-second go/no-go") and is worse on the phone-in-hand, morning-of use case the product is built for. Fix: raise the base/data-label font size to at least 12–14px; audit the bottom bar and card metadata specifically. Suggested command: `/impeccable typeset`

**[P1] Text contrast fails WCAG AA on brand-relevant elements.** Detector-confirmed: `h2.title` at 2.5:1 (#6c8ef5 on light background) and `.route-cluster` badges at 2.8:1 (white on #7a97f2) — both below the 4.5:1 minimum. Why it matters: this is the app's own primary accent color and its map-cluster labels, i.e. the most-seen text in the app, not an edge case. Fix: darken the accent for text use or lighten/darken the surrounding surface until 4.5:1 is met; re-check with the detector after. Suggested command: `/impeccable audit`

**[P1] Search and detail panels overlap instead of taking turns.** Confirmed live: opening the Search card while a route/station detail panel is open does not close the detail panel (`App.vue`'s `activePanel` and `detailItem` are independent refs; `openSearch()` never clears `detailItem`), so the two absolutely-positioned cards visually collide on desktop. Why it matters: "check a route, then check search again to compare another" is the primary task loop, and it's the one place two of the app's own floating-panel systems fight each other. Fix: make opening one clear the other (mirroring what `selectWaterStationFromSearch`/`openRouteDetail` already do), or dock search instead of floating it when a detail card is open. Suggested command: `/impeccable layout`

**[P2] Any open panel occludes the map markers behind it.** Detector-confirmed: opening the route detail or settings panel covers ~20 numbered map markers 42–92% by the panel's own opaque content. This compounds a related design gap: routes, water stations, and rainfall stations all share one Leaflet cluster layer with no per-kind breakdown, so a user already can't tell "3 routes + 14 stations" from "17 water stations" at a glance — and now the panel meant to answer that question can bury the very markers being asked about. Fix: keep panels clear of the working map area (or auto-pan the map to the visible remainder), and consider splitting cluster groups by marker kind. Suggested command: `/impeccable layout`

**[P2] A route-fetch failure takes down the entire app, not just the list.** `App.vue`'s top-level `loadError` overlay replaces map + sidebar + everything with a centered retry message when PocketBase is unreachable — far harsher than the scoped per-card retry pattern already used well in `WaterStationDetail`/`RainfallStationDetail`. Why it matters: Product Principle #1 asks to be explicit when an API is down, but this makes even the *map* and cached content unavailable during a route-service outage, which is more disruptive than the failure warrants. Fix: reuse the existing scoped retry-card pattern instead of a full-screen blocker. Suggested command: `/impeccable harden`

## Persona Red Flags

**Casey (mobile, morning-of, one-handed, low patience)**: `RouteDetail.vue` switches to a bottom sheet under 640px, but `WaterStationDetail`/`RainfallStationDetail` switch at 600px — on a 601–640px-wide device, one panel type floats as a centered card and the other becomes a bottom sheet in the *same session*, breaking the mental model on exactly the device class this app targets. (Live mobile-width rendering could not be confirmed in either sub-agent's browser session — this is a source-code finding, flagged as such.) Combined with the confirmed 10.4px text and sub-4.5:1 contrast above, Casey is the persona hit hardest by this run's findings.

**Jordan (first-timer, doesn't know if V3/A2/III is "fine for them")**: Grade tooltips are hover/focus-only with no visual affordance (no underline, no icon) suggesting a pill is tappable — a first-timer sees three cryptic codes with no signal that an explanation exists. The Grading Guide is one click away but not linked contextually from the grade pills themselves.

**Sam (accessibility-dependent, screen reader/keyboard)**: The grade tooltips (`ROPE_TIPS`/`WATER_TIPS`/`TIME_TIPS`) render via `data-tooltip` + CSS `::after` content, which is invisible to screen readers — so the actual safety explanation behind a grade code doesn't reach Sam at all, compounding the low-contrast finding above (`h2.title`, cluster badges) on the same visit. On the positive side, focus-visible outlines are applied consistently across nearly every interactive element reviewed.

## Minor Observations

- `CanyonList.vue`'s route row packs 7 data points (name, drop, V/A/T pills, star pill, region) into one line — over the ≤4-chunk guideline for the first thing scanned in a pre-trip comparison. Worth revisiting alongside the P1 text-size fix above.
- `SearchCard.vue` shows 13 controls at once (4 scope + 5 region + 4 grade/drop selects + GPX checkbox) with no primary/secondary grouping — a single decision point well over the ≤4-option guidance.
- The flagged `clipped-overflow-container`/`cramped-padding` on `.app-layout`/`.sidebar-wrap`/`#map` and the "0.67px border" are false positives (fixed-position element not actually clipped; Leaflet's own required full-bleed container; devicePixelRatio measurement artifact on a real `1px solid` border) — no action needed.
- `App.vue` duplicates the same card-position math in two places (~line 330 and ~line 387) — maintenance risk, not user-facing.
- The light theme reads as "dark theme inverted" rather than a separately designed second brand expression — fine for now, worth a look if a real visual-identity pass happens later.

## Questions to Consider

1. The app's best feature — the water-station verdict card — is one navigation step behind a map icon or a search flow. Should the nearest station's verdict show inline in `RouteDetail` itself, given the whole product's job is a single 30-second read?
2. Given three data types already share one cluster layer, is a bare cluster count ever the right answer to "what's near this route," or should selecting a route always resolve stations by distance/list rather than by map cluster?
